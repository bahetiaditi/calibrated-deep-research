A claim in a draft report failed verification. Decide what to do about it.

Five options. The choice is a judgment about WHY it failed, and the reason
decides which one is right.

  RE_RETRIEVE   The claim is probably true and the right passage was simply
                not found. The sub-question is sound, the evidence looks
                near-miss rather than absent. Give a better query.
  REVISE_PLAN   The SUB-QUESTION is the problem — too vague, too broad, or
                asking something no source would state in those terms. No
                query against it will work until it is rewritten.
  DROP_CLAIM    The evidence suggests the claim is simply WRONG. Not
                unsupported — contradicted. The report is better without it,
                and the error is worth recording.
  HEDGE         The evidence is genuinely mixed: sources disagree, or the
                passage supports a weaker version than the claim states.
                Keep it, state the disagreement, cite both sides.
  UNRESOLVED    Nothing further will help. Retrieval has been tried and
                failed, or the corpus does not contain this. Report the gap.

How to tell them apart, since this is where the judgment lives:

  RE_RETRIEVE vs UNRESOLVED — has retrieval actually been tried properly for
  this claim? Look at the attempted queries. If they were all phrased one
  way and the corpus would phrase it another, re-retrieve. If several
  genuinely different queries have failed, the corpus does not have it.

  RE_RETRIEVE vs REVISE_PLAN — would a better QUERY fix it, or does the
  SUB-QUESTION itself need rewriting? "What rank did they use?" retrieves
  badly because it is vague about who and what; no query fixes that.

  DROP_CLAIM vs HEDGE — does the evidence say the claim is false, or that it
  is one of two defensible positions? Dropping a contested-but-defensible
  claim loses information; hedging a false one launders it.

Bias: when RE_RETRIEVE and UNRESOLVED are both arguable, prefer UNRESOLVED.
An admitted gap is a usable result; a third failed retrieval is spent budget
and the same gap. When DROP and HEDGE are both arguable, prefer HEDGE —
removing a claim removes the reader's chance to judge it.

For RE_RETRIEVE, give the query. It must differ substantially from every
attempted query listed; a rephrasing that returns the same passages is a
retry, not a retrieval.
