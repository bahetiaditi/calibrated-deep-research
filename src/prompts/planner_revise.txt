A sub-question is failing. Retrieval has been tried and has not produced
usable evidence. Decide what to do with it.

Four options. The right one depends on WHY it is failing, and the
sub-question's stated purpose is what tells you.

  REWRITE  The sub-question asks the right thing in the wrong words. It is
           vague about which entity, which quantity, or which setting — so
           no query against it can be specific. Rewrite it to ask the same
           thing precisely.
  SPLIT    It asks for two or more things at once. Each part is answerable
           alone; together they retrieve a muddle. Break it into 2-3
           sub-questions that each have one answer.
  MERGE    Another sub-question in the plan already covers this ground. The
           evidence it needs will come from that one's retrieval, so this
           one is duplicated effort.
  ABANDON  The sub-question is sound and the corpus does not contain the
           answer. No rewording will help.

How to tell them apart:

  REWRITE vs ABANDON — would a specific version of this question be
  answerable from the sources available? "What rank did they use?" is vague
  and fixable. "What did the authors privately think?" is precise and
  unanswerable. Look at what retrieval DID return: near-misses on the right
  topic mean the question is findable and badly phrased; nothing on the
  topic at all means it is probably not there.

  REWRITE vs SPLIT — does the question have one answer or several? If a
  correct answer would be two sentences about two different things, split
  it.

  MERGE — only when another sub-question genuinely subsumes this one. Two
  sub-questions about the same paper are not the same sub-question.

Bias toward ABANDON when REWRITE is a guess. A third rewrite of a question
the corpus cannot answer spends budget to arrive at the same gap, and an
honest gap is a usable result. Rewrite when you can point at what was vague;
abandon when you cannot.

For REWRITE, the new text must be answerable from a passage, name every
entity explicitly, and differ substantially from the original — a
rephrasing that retrieves the same passages is not a revision.

For SPLIT, each part must stand alone with no reference to the others.

For MERGE, name the sub-question that covers it.
